CodeMetaSoft

Detecting, reporting and fixing research software metadata drift
w3id.org/codemetasoft

Summary

Your codemeta.json was right the day you wrote it. Is it still?

Software metadata gets written once and then left to rot. We built a checker that compares codemeta.json with the rest of the repository, a catalogue of 29 issues, and a bot and CI action that report them.

On 1,500 repositories that already ship the file, most issues are about time: versions, dates and dependencies that were right once and never updated. The gap is maintenance, not adoption.

A typical codemeta.json, one release later
{
  "version": "1.1.0",P001
  "license": "GPL",P013
  "identifier": "10.5281/zenodo.123",P014
  "dateModified": "2022-03-01",W002
  "softwareRequirements": ["numpy"]W001
}
Illustrative file. The latest release is 1.2.0, the license needs a version, the DOI is not a URL, the file has not changed since 2022, and numpy has no version.

Challenge

  • Software metadata (authors, version, license, identifiers, dependencies) makes research software findable, citable and reusable.
  • It is spread over many files: pyproject.toml, pom.xml, DESCRIPTION, CITATION.cff, README, LICENSE. CodeMeta gathers it in one codemeta.json, written by hand.
  • FAIR checkers test whether a field is present. None checked whether its value is correct or agrees with the rest of the repository.
  • Adoption is low too: only 0.13% of 370,684 OpenAIRE software repositories have a codemeta.json.

Metadata practices in the Science Clusters (MSR 2025), the OpenAIRE Graph and 1,500 CodeMeta repositories.
Diagnose
Two authors and two experts inspected 150 random repositories by hand.

A CI check for developers; a bot and a dashboard for registries and labs.

The checker is accurate

Measured against a published hand-annotated ground truth (rsmetacheck 0.3.5, 143 repositories):

0.89precision: 358 of 403 findings are right 0.80recall: 0.88 without W004, a rule we relaxed after maintainer feedback

Curation helps, but not over time

The ESCAPE OSSR (30 particle physics and astronomy projects) requires a codemeta.json at submission. Even there:

1.0pitfall per record on average 1.4warnings per record on average

53% list requirements without versions, 40% have a stale dateModified. The onboarding review catches broken links; drift escapes it.

Monitoring runs, behaviour does not change

The bot ran 50 times unattended on the OEG-UPM organisation (331 → 341 repositories, Jul–Sep 2026). Pitfalls per repository went from 0.060 to 0.070, warnings from 0.193 to 0.202. An issue alone does not change practice.

Exploitation of results

  • ESCAPE OSSR runs value checks and a public monitoring page on top of its CodeMeta completeness indicator.
  • EVERSE: reports follow its assessment schema; LAPP's completeness indicator is registered there.
  • FAIR2ADAPT shows rsmetacheck reports in its software catalogue.
  • Nothing is physics-specific: any lab or registry can point the bot at its repositories.

What we learned

Check values, not fields. Comparing codemeta.json with manifests, releases and CITATION.cff finds what presence checks miss.
Policy catches structure, not drift. Versions and dates go stale after the submission review.
Fixes should arrive at release time, as pull requests, when people care about metadata.

Next: auto-fix pull requests for the easy cases, rsmetacheck at OSSR submission, Software Heritage integration.

Sustainability

All tools are open source and run without us. Every catalogue entry has a persistent identifier, e.g. w3id.org/rsmetacheck/catalog/#P001, with a definition, a real example and a fix.
